The Cross-Border Cost Brief

What your Ontario lanes cost, every month, on one page

Fuel, currency and border delay for US carriers running Ontario to Michigan or New York. Built from the primary sources, dated, and put on a single basis so you can quote from it.

What is in it

  1. Cost per 1,000 km on each side. Canadian diesel and US diesel converted to the same basis at a 38 L/100 km tractor, so the two numbers are directly comparable. You are not doing arithmetic mid-quote.
  2. The spread you are quoting against. The difference between the two headline prices on a common basis, in cents per litre, stated with its direction.
  3. Provincial diesel. Where you actually fuel before the return leg, ranked against the national average.
  4. Your crossings. Canada-side and US-side booth waits at Windsor-Detroit, Sarnia-Port Huron, Fort Erie-Buffalo, Queenston-Lewiston and Sault Ste. Marie.
  5. What to carry into a quote this month. One paragraph, no hedging.

Why it is not just a diesel price

You can get a US diesel price anywhere. What you cannot get in one place is the Canadian provincial figure next to the US district figure, converted to what a tractor actually burns per kilometre, with the border delay on the crossings your drivers use.

A fuel card tells you what you paid. This tells you what to charge.

Where the numbers come from

Canadian diesel, by provinceNatural Resources Canada weekly retail survey
US diesel, by districtUS Energy Information Administration
USD/CADBank of Canada daily observation
Border wait timesCanada Border Services Agency, US Customs and Border Protection
Fuel tax by jurisdictionIFTA Inc. quarterly tax matrix

Every figure carries its observation date. Where a source has not published, the report says so rather than carrying the previous value forward silently. The method is public at the methodology page.
