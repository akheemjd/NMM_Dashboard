How it is calculated

What drives Manitoba prices

Manitoba is the smallest survey on this site: two cities, Winnipeg and Brandon, and they are close enough together that the provincial average is nearly an Edmonton-to-Winnipeg freight story rather than a provincial one. In the Natural Resources Canada print dated 29 September 2026 the province averaged 246.9 cents per litre, 14.0 cents below the national index of 260.9, which puts it among the cheapest in the country.

The reason is position. Manitoba sits between the Alberta refining complex and the Ontario market, and a good deal of fuel moving east through the province does not stop there. Winnipeg is a distribution point on the way to somewhere else, and a market that exists partly to pass product through tends to price closer to the wholesale side than a market that exists only to consume.

The thinness of the sample is worth understanding before you use the number. Two cities is not a provincial picture. It is Winnipeg and Brandon, and a carrier running the Trans-Canada west of Portage la Prairie or the northern route to Thompson is not buying in either. What the figure is genuinely good for is direction. If Manitoba's average moves, Winnipeg moved, and Winnipeg is where most of the province's fuel is bought.

One caution on every figure here. The NRCan survey measures posted retail rather than cardlock, so your actual cost is usually below the posted average. Plan a specific fill from your card price and use this number to see which way the province is heading.
