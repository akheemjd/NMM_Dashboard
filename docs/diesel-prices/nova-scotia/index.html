What drives Nova Scotia prices

Nova Scotia sits close to the national average, which for an Atlantic province is worth explaining. In the Natural Resources Canada print dated 29 September 2026 it averaged 266.4 cents per litre, 5.5 cents above the national index of 260.9, a smaller gap than either New Brunswick or Newfoundland.

Halifax is the reason. It is a deep-water port with an import terminal and the largest population centre in the region, and a port that can take a marine cargo directly does not pay the inland haul that a smaller market does. Sydney and the Cape Breton markets are further from that terminal and price accordingly. Kentville, New Glasgow, Truro and Yarmouth fill in the rest of a six-city survey that covers the province fairly well for its size.

The pattern to watch here is the relationship with New Brunswick rather than the national average. The two provinces share a supply corridor and a border, and when they diverge it is usually because of what happened at Saint John or on the water rather than anything local. For a carrier running Halifax to Moncton, the spread between those two figures is the number that matters, not either one of them against the country.

One caution on every figure here. The NRCan survey measures posted retail, not cardlock. Your fuel card price is usually lower, so plan a specific fill from your card and use this number to track which way the province is going.
