Across the line

Diesel crosses the border, but the number does not. The Canadian side is published per litre and the American side per gallon.

The American figure is the EIA weekly retail survey — published as $6.476/gal this week — shown the way an American carrier reads it, with the Canadian-dollar equivalent beside it once the page loads. A gallon is not a litre, so these two lines measure the same fuel in different units and the cents will never match. What does compare is the market: a load crossing into New England buys its fuel on the other side of a border.

How it is calculated

What drives Halifax prices

Halifax is the largest city in Atlantic Canada and runs a major container and auto port. It has had no local refinery since the Dartmouth plant closed, so its fuel is imported. Imported fuel keeps prices high. That is the position, and it holds steady. The port moves containers and cars, but the city has no plant making diesel of its own. The price at the pump carries the cost of bringing fuel in from elsewhere, and that cost does not go away.
