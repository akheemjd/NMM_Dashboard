The four numbers add up. State excise $0.2300 plus other state fees $0.0003 gives a state total of $0.2303. Add the federal $0.2440 and the all-in figure is $0.4743 a gallon.

Under IFTA, a US-licensed carrier files North Dakota at $0.2300 a gallon for diesel. That is what the state actually collects on a gallon sold there, and it is a different number from the statutory total because IFTA and the statute do not measure the same thing.

The two figures above answer different questions. The statutory excise is what North Dakota levies on a gallon of diesel sold in the state. The IFTA rate is what a carrier reports to North Dakota for a litre burned there, and in most jurisdictions the two are not the same number.

Why there is no North Dakota diesel price here

The Energy Information Administration publishes a weekly on-highway diesel price on a district basis, not a state basis. There are eleven districts in the whole country and North Dakota is not one of them.

North Dakota sits in the Midwest (PADD 2) district. The figure at the top of this page is that district's weekly price. It is not a North Dakota price, and we are not going to label it as one.

Plenty of sites will show you a North Dakota diesel price. What they are showing you is the same district number they show for every neighbouring state, because the state-level number does not exist to show. If a site claims a per-state diesel price, ask it which survey it came from.
