# Northern Mile Media — Content Samples & References

## 1. NMDI Model: How Named Indices Write

The S&P Global PMI is the closest model for how the NMDI should read. No hedging. Flat statement. Named series.

**S&P Global PMI sample:**
> The S&P Global Canada Manufacturing PMI posted 47.5 in July, down from 48.8 in June. The reading signalled a solid deterioration in operating conditions.

**What NMDI should look like:**
> Northern Mile Diesel Index, Ontario: 171.2¢/L. Down 0.3¢ on the week. Seven provinces below the national average for the fourth consecutive week.

Pattern: Index name → number → change → what it means.

## 2. LinkedIn Business Page: How TruckNews Writes

TruckNews dominates LinkedIn with story-first, data-second posts. Every post names a real carrier or event.

**TruckNews sample (Gordie Howe bridge):**
> Onfreight Logistics will be the first commercial carrier to transport live freight across the Gordie Howe International Bridge when it opens to commercial traffic on July 27. The Tecumseh, Ont.-based carrier said the milestone reflects more than two decades of advocacy by president Steve Ondejko Sr.

**What NMM should look like (Tuesday — Story + Data):**
> Onfreight Logistics ran the first commercial load across the Gordie Howe bridge this week. Ambassador Bridge has owned Windsor-Detroit for decades. That changes now. The NMDI shows diesel at 171.2 in Ontario and 165.7 in Manitoba. Dispatchers routing this corridor just got an option they never had. Here is what a second crossing does to routing math.

Pattern: Real event → our data → operational implication.

## 4. Personal LinkedIn: How Industry Founders Post

The best personal posts are observations, not reports. Casual. First person. No CTA.

**Good personal post (model):**
> Just checked the dashboard. NMDI shows diesel at 171.9 for the eighth straight week. That is not normal for July. Usually demand pushes it up by now. Curious if anyone else is seeing this in their lanes.

**Bad personal post (avoid):**
> This week in Canadian trucking, we analyzed diesel price trends across all ten provinces and found that...

Pattern: "I noticed" → the number → why it's interesting → open question.

## 5. Blog Post: The FreightWaves Deep Dive

FreightWaves weekly analysis posts follow a strict format: headline stat → context → data → implication → what to do.

**Sample structure:**
```
## Diesel Spread Hits 23.6 Cents. What That Costs on a Real Run.

[Lead with the number. No throat clearing.]

## The numbers
[Province breakdown. Table or list. Scannable.]

[Translate spread to dollars on specific lanes.]
```

## 6. Newsletter: The Axios Model

Axios and Morning Brew are the model for short, scannable newsletters with personality.

**Pattern per section:**
> ## The loonie keeps grinding
> USD/CAD touched 1.3996 this week. Down from 1.42 a month ago. A $5,000 US load was $7,105 then. Same load today is $6,991. Five cross-border hauls a week and the math quietly took $535 off your revenue.

Pattern: One stat → translate to dollars → one sentence of what to do.

---

## Universal Rules (from all winners)

1. **Number first.** Never open with context. Open with the number.
2. **Translate to dollars.** Every stat, every time.
3. **One point per section.** A newsletter with 4 sections should leave you with 4 things you can act on.
4. **No hedging.** "NMDI at 171.2" not "Diesel estimated at approximately 171.2."
5. **Short.** The best newsletter took 90 seconds to read. The best LinkedIn post was 140 words.
