Quebec pays more for diesel than any other province in the Natural Resources Canada survey. In the print dated 29 September 2026 the province averaged 291.4 cents per litre, 30.5 cents above the national index of 260.9. That is not a rounding difference or a seasonal spike. It has held across prints.

Quebec's tax stack is a large part of it. The province applies a fuel tax and the Quebec sales tax on top, a different structure from the one most provinces use, and the combined effective rate is among the highest in the country. Tax is the one part of the pump price a carrier can plan around, because it does not move with the crude market.

The survey covers ten Quebec cities and the internal spread is narrower than Ontario's, which tells you something about how the province is supplied. Montreal and Quebec City anchor the St Lawrence corridor with the Lévis refinery between them, and most of the province's population sits along that line. Gatineau is close to the Ontario border and to Ottawa's supply, while Chicoutimi, Rimouski and Gaspé are at the end of long hauls, and Val-d'Or and Trois-Rivières sit between.

For a cross-border carrier this is the number to compare against Ontario rather than against Canada. A load from Toronto to Montreal crosses a fuel market boundary that matters more than the provincial line suggests.
