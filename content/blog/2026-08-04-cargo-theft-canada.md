$5.2 million. Eight incidents. April to July.

The GTA led the count. Four thefts in Brampton and Mississauga alone. Most were yard intrusions. Fence cuts. Unlocked trailers. One reefer disappeared from a truck stop on Dixie Road while the driver was inside.

Montreal posted the biggest single loss. A coordinated ring hit multiple yards in Saint-Laurent and Anjou. Police recovered 47 trailers. $4 million total. Recovered, not prevented.

What is changing is the pickup fraud. Calgary and Delta, BC both saw fictitious pickups this spring. Someone showed up with stolen carrier credentials and paperwork that looked real. The Calgary load was worth $90,000. Delta was $180,000. Both left the yard without anyone making a phone call to verify the carrier.

Alberta's industrial parks are active. Nisku lost three reefers on a weekend in April. Fence cut. Trucks driven out. Nobody noticed until Monday. $300,000.

Winnipeg's CentrePort area is emerging. Five trailers in April. All unlocked. No forced entry needed.

What stops this.

GPS tracking with geofence alerts caught four of the eight thefts. A trailer moves after hours and someone gets notified. That is not prevention, but it speeds recovery. Most of the stolen loads were recovered within 48 hours when GPS was active. The ones without it took weeks or were never found.

King pin locks and gladhand locks. A $30 lock stops a trailer from coupling. The five Winnipeg trailers had neither. Eight hundred dollars in locks across the fleet would have saved $210,000. The Montreal ring targeted yards where trailers sat unsecured overnight. A lock on every trailer and those 47 units stay put.

Weekend patrols. Three of eight incidents happened on Saturday or Sunday. Yards that ran security on weekends did not appear on this list. The Nisku theft was a weekend hit through a cut fence. Three reefers gone. Two full days before anyone knew.

Carrier verification. A phone call to a publicly listed number stops a fictitious pickup. Not the number on the email. Not the one on the carrier packet that just arrived. The one on the carrier's actual website.

The hotspots are stable. GTA. Montreal. Vancouver's port and Delta. Calgary's Balzac and Foothills. Edmonton's Nisku and Acheson. Winnipeg is new. If you park in any of these zones overnight, lock your equipment and turn on geofence alerts. A phone call and a padlock cover most of what happened here.
