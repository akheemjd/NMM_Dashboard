# Diesel is in the top three per cent of the last five years, and the honest forecast is no change

Analysis of the national weekly print, 566 readings from January 2016 to 3 November 2026.

## Where it sits

The latest print is 258.1 cents a litre. A year ago the same survey said 160.1. Over ten years the series has run from 86.7 to 270.5, and the current reading sits at the 97th percentile of the last 260 weekly prints.

That is the whole setup. A carrier pricing a lane this week is pricing fuel at a level it has seen in about three per cent of the last five years, during a year in which the print has moved nearly a dollar.

## The calendar is already at the top

Averaged across ten years, the two dearest months of the year are October at 154.5 and November at 152.9. The two cheapest are January at 142.3 and June at 142.8.

So the seasonal argument for waiting does not exist this month. The expensive part of the calendar is where we are standing, and the historical average says the next easing, if it comes, is a first-quarter story.

## What we tested, and what won

Five models, all walked forward over the last 104 prints with only the data that existed at the time. Mean absolute error in cents per litre, and the share of prints called within 2 cents.

| model | MAE | bias | within 2c |
|---|---|---|---|
| last print | 3.72 | -0.87 | 46% |
| last print plus 13 week drift | 4.27 | -0.07 | 40% |
| mean of last 4 | 7.15 | -2.26 | 32% |
| mean of last 8 | 10.71 | -4.16 | 15% |
| same week last year | 34.42 | -15.19 | 7% |

The random walk wins. Assume next week looks like this week and you beat every smoothing model on this data. The drift model removes the bias and still loses on error, which says the series has no usable momentum at a one week horizon.

The seasonal model is the interesting failure. Asking what this week cost a year ago misses by an average of 34.42 cents, because it assumes the level repeats and last year the level was 160.

## The forecast for 10 November

258.1 cents a litre, inside a band of 252.4 to 263.8.

The band is 11.4 cents wide, which on a 400 litre fill is 46 dollars of uncertainty in one purchase. That is the number worth sitting with. A forecast that cannot narrow the question below 46 dollars a fill is telling you to price the lane on the high end and move on.

## What gets published next

This forecast is written to a ledger before the print exists. When the 10 November survey lands it gets scored against the actual, and the error goes on a public record next to every previous one, wins and misses.

That is the product. Not the prediction. The scoreboard that makes the prediction worth reading.
