# NMM Blog — Content Strategy

**Cadence:** 3 posts/week (Mon / Wed / Fri)
**Started:** September 2026
**Goal:** Rank for Canadian trucking search terms and grow newsletter subscribers

---

## Why 3/week

- **1/week** is too slow to build topical authority. Google needs volume plus consistency.
- **7/week** would force thin content. We do not have seven distinct data stories a week,
  and thin content suppresses rankings rather than helping them.
- **3/week** matches what the data can actually support. Each data stream has a different
  cadence (diesel weekly, FX daily, borders live), which maps onto three distinct post types.

Consistency beats volume. A predictable Mon/Wed/Fri schedule that never breaks
outperforms a burst followed by silence.

---

## The three pillars

### PILLAR 1 — Market Pulse (Monday)
*"What the numbers did"*

Timely recap of the dashboard's live data. Short and scannable, 700–1,100 words.

Uses: NRCan diesel print, Bank of Canada FX, CBSA border waits, EIA US diesel,
freight barometer readings.

Target searchers: people typing "canada diesel price this week", "usd cad rate trucking".

### PILLAR 2 — Deep Dive (Wednesday)
*"One number, taken apart"*

A single dataset analysed properly. Longer, 1,200–2,000 words.

Uses: provincial diesel comparisons, single-crossing history, cost-per-kilometre math,
seasonal patterns, Canada vs US diesel gap, excise tax arithmetic.

Target searchers: long-tail queries. "diesel price alberta vs quebec",
"ambassador bridge wait times by hour", "cost per km trucking canada".

### PILLAR 3 — Rules & Money (Friday)
*"The regulation that costs you money"*

The proven lane. Explains a rule, then shows the arithmetic of compliance vs penalties.
1,000–1,600 words.

Uses: CRA / PSB rules, Driver Inc, IFTA, hours of service, excise tax changes,
tariffs and trade policy, CBSA and customs procedure, cargo theft liability, ELD mandates.

Target searchers: people who search a specific rule because it is threatening them.
High intent, low competition, converts to newsletter subscribers.

**Why this pillar matters most:** the three best-performing posts on the blog are all
from this category. Regulatory explainers are the differentiator — anyone can repost
a diesel price, almost nobody explains the CRA's PSB test in plain language.

---

## Standing rules for every post

1. **Every number comes from the data brief.** Never compute, estimate, or invent.
   If the brief does not have it, the post does not claim it.
2. **Attribute to the named source** — Natural Resources Canada, Bank of Canada,
   CBSA, EIA. Never "according to reports".
3. **Link to 3–5 dashboard pages.** There are 98 to choose from. Deep links
   (a specific city or crossing) beat generic ones.
4. **Primary keyword** in the title, first 100 words, one H2, and the closing paragraph.
5. **Soft CTA** at the end pointing back to the dashboard. No hard sells.
6. **Voice:** dispatcher talking to a trucker. Short sentences. No AI-speak.
7. **The dashboard links back.** Every post should be reachable from the dashboard,
   or the internal link equity flows one way and dies.

---

## Growth levers beyond frequency

Publishing more only helps if these are also moving:

| Lever | State | Action |
|-------|-------|--------|
| Internal links | 98 dashboard pages, underused | 3–5 per post, deep links |
| Backlinks | Targets listed, **unsent** | Send Tier 1 emails — AMTA, OTA, CTA, BCTA |
| Newsletter | 1 subscriber | Wire the subscribe CTA into every post |
| Distribution | Nothing automated | LinkedIn + relevant subreddits per post |

**The single highest-leverage action available right now is the unsent backlink emails.**
Five emails to AMTA, OTA, CTA, BCTA and FleetOwner. Free, high authority, directly
relevant. That is worth more than a month of extra posts.

---

## Realistic timeline

- **Months 1–3:** indexing and sandbox. Low traffic is normal. Do not panic.
- **Months 3–6:** long-tail keywords start ranking. Newsletter growth begins.
- **Month 6+:** compounding. Older posts accumulate links and lift the whole domain.
