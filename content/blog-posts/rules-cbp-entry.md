A load rarely gets held at the US border because the driver did something wrong. It gets held because a form the driver never touches was filed late, filed wrong, or never filed at all. That is what CBP entry filing for trucking comes down to. Somebody in an office three states away files the paper. The truck carries the consequence.

US Customs and Border Protection runs the American side the way the Canada Border Services Agency runs the Canadian side. The difference is who owns the documents. On a US-bound load, the driver is a witness. Not the filer.

## What CBP entry filing for trucking covers

Three documents, three parties. Mixing them up is how loads end up parked.

The carrier files the truck manifest. Under 19 CFR 123.92, CBP has to receive the cargo information electronically before the truck reaches its first port of arrival in the United States. The regulation sets that window at 30 minutes ahead on some approved systems and an hour ahead on others. Not at the booth. Before it.

The importer of record files the entry. CBP Form 3461 is the entry and immediate delivery document, the one that asks CBP to release the goods. CBP Form 7501 is the entry summary, where the classification and the duty get declared. Under 19 CFR 142.2, merchandise has to be entered within 15 calendar days of arrival, and that clock runs whether the truck has moved on or not.

A licensed customs broker normally files both on the importer's behalf. That is the person the driver never meets and the person who decides whether the load rolls.

## The clock on a load that files clean

The board makes the good days look easy. The [Peace Bridge](https://dashboard.northernmilemedia.com/border-wait-times/peace-bridge/) sat at 13 minutes in [CBSA's crossing data](https://dashboard.northernmilemedia.com/border-wait-times/) this week, with Queenston-Lewiston at 7 and the Pacific Highway Crossing at 5. Ambassador Bridge, Blue Water Bridge, Lacolle, Coutts-Sweetgrass and Emerson-Pembina all sat clear. Three or more ports carried a delay, and the rest did not.

Those are waits to enter Canada. They are the Canadian mirror of the American booth, and the US side runs its own queue with its own numbers.

## The arithmetic of compliance against penalty

Doing it right is boring and priced. A broker fee per entry. A customs bond, either a continuous bond that covers the year or a single entry bond bought per shipment. The labour to build a correct file before the truck rolls.

Doing it wrong sits in 19 U.S.C. 1592. That is the civil penalty statute, and it grades violations by culpability. Negligence is one class. Gross negligence is another. Fraud is the worst. CBP issues a pre-penalty notice naming the class it alleges, and the amount is set against that class and the duty owed.

Here is the part that matters at the wheel. That dollar exposure lands on the importer of record. It does not land on the carrier. The carrier's exposure is simpler. A truck hooked to a load CBP has not released is a truck that is not moving and not earning.

| What goes wrong | Who carries it | What the carrier feels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manifest transmitted late | Carrier | Truck held at the port |
| Wrong classification on the entry | Importer of record | Release denied, cargo sits |
| Missing agency data on the file | Importer and broker | Exam ordered, or entry refused |
| No bond, or a bond too small | Importer | CBP will not release |
| A classification dispute | Importer | The load waits on the dock |

## The currency side of a cross border invoice

The loonie sat at 1.4196 against the US dollar on 15 October, down 0.5 per cent. Crude is priced in US dollars, so a stronger Canadian dollar softens the cost of the barrel when it lands here. The mechanism runs in reverse when the loonie slips.

For a carrier billing an American shipper in US dollars, the rate is part of the negotiation, not background noise. The US Energy Information Administration printed American diesel at 6.179 dollars a gallon on 12 October. That figure stays in dollars per gallon. Canadian figures stay in cents per litre. Litres against gallons and cents against dollars do not convert cleanly into one number, so they do not get seated side by side here.

## What to check before the wheels turn

Five things, and every one of them is cheaper than a hold.

Confirm the broker has the commercial invoice and the packing list early, not on the morning of the pickup. Confirm the truck manifest goes in ahead of arrival, inside the window 19 CFR 123.92 sets. Confirm the bond covers the shipment. Confirm any agency data the commodity needs, because food, vehicles and chemicals drag extra agencies into the file. Confirm the classification with the broker in writing before the load is sealed.

None of it is the driver's job. All of it lands on the driver's clock when it fails.

CBP entry filing for trucking rewards the boring habit of getting the paper right before the truck rolls.
