# Data Hub: what we build, and what it costs

*A one-page offer. Northern Mile Media is the reference build.*

---

## The problem we solve

Your industry has data your members or readers care about, and it lives in
scattered PDF releases, government tables and spreadsheets nobody opens.

You have probably already decided you should publish it. What stopped you is the
same thing that stops everyone: a live data property is not a web project, it is
an operations problem. Somebody has to collect it, clean it, notice when a feed
breaks, regenerate the charts, rewrite the pages, keep it indexed, and do it
again next week, forever. Most organisations buy the build and quietly abandon
the thing eight months later when the person who maintained it leaves.

We have solved that problem, and we can point at a live one.

## The reference build

dashboard.northernmilemedia.com: a free Canadian trucking data property.

| | |
|---|---|
| Pages published | 108 |
| Primary sources | Natural Resources Canada, Canada Border Services Agency, Bank of Canada, US Energy Information Administration, Statistics Canada |
| Refresh | Every 30 minutes, unattended |
| Code | 56 scripts, 13,465 lines, 20 page templates |
| Human input to publish | None |
| Editorial output | Articles and a weekly newsletter, generated, gated and sent automatically |

What it does without anyone touching it:

- Collects from five primary sources and normalises them into one schema
- Generates 108 pages: 69 city pages, ten provinces, five US regions, nine
  commercial border crossings
- Renders charts, share cards and structured data per page
- Writes, lints and publishes articles, then emails them
- Deploys itself and re-submits its own sitemap

And the part that actually matters: it refuses to publish a number it cannot
verify. Four independent guards run on every build and stop the deploy on a
broken link, an orphaned page, a missing data source, or a figure that
contradicts another page on the same site. Every figure carries its source and
its print date.

That last property is the whole product. Anyone can put up a chart. Publishing a
number a professional will cite, and standing behind it, is the hard part.

## What you get

Build (weeks 1 to 6)

1. Source audit. Which feeds exist, which are stable, which revise after
   publication, which will silently die.
2. A normalisation layer, so every source lands in one schema.
3. The page system, built around the questions your audience actually searches.
4. Charts matched to the shape of the data, not one uniform template.
5. A self-correcting publish path: sources, citations, structured data, sitemap,
   share cards.
6. The guards. This is the deliverable that keeps it alive in year two.
7. Your team trained on the one file they need to understand to add a source.

Operate (ongoing)

We run it. Feed monitoring, breakage alerts, source changes, revisions, and a
monthly note on what moved and what broke. You get a property that stays true
without employing anyone to maintain it.

## How this differs from hiring an agency

An agency builds you a site and leaves. You then own a thing that decays, and the
decay is invisible until a number is wrong in public.

We hand over something that checks itself, and we stay on the hook for it. The
guards are the point. A data property without them is a liability with a nice
chart on it.

## What we need from you

- The data. Public feeds, or your own records.
- One person inside your organisation who knows the industry and can be
  consulted, not a project manager.
- A decision on what the property is for: member value, lead generation, or
  public authority. It changes the page architecture.

## Commercial shape

Two numbers: a build fee, and a monthly operating fee.

The build fee reflects scope, mainly how many sources and how many page
families. The operating fee is deliberately low, because the entire design goal
is that it runs without us once it is up.

Rates are set per engagement and we will quote yours in writing before any work
starts. We do not publish a rate card here, because the honest number depends
on your sources and your page families, and a public range would just be a
number we walk back later.
