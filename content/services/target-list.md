# Target list: data hub engagements

Read this first. `web_search` is down on billing, so I could not verify what
these engagements sell for, who is currently buying, or the contact details for
anyone below Tier 1. The Tier 1 contacts were verified in an earlier pass.
Everything else is a starting point that needs a two-minute check before you
send anything.

The point of the list is the shape of the buyer, not the names. Any industry
with data its members care about and no staff to publish it is a target. Trucking
is where you have proof, not where the market ends.

---

## Tier 1: Canadian trucking associations

Verified working. The strongest fit because you can hand them a live example in
their own industry.

| Organisation | Contact | Why them |
|---|---|---|
| Alberta Motor Transport Association | info@amta.ca | Verified links page, provincial body |
| Ontario Trucking Association | info@ontruck.org | Largest provincial association |
| Canadian Trucking Alliance | contact form | National umbrella, provincial members follow |
| BC Trucking Association | info@bctrucking.com | Provincial body |
| Trucking HR Canada | info@truckinghr.com | Government-funded, publishes workforce data |

Do not send these to `info@` and hope. Find a named person first. Association
sites list staff and directors, and a named email roughly triples the reply rate.

Reframe the ask. These were the backlink targets. A backlink costs them
effort and earns you nothing. A member-facing data property is something they
already want and cannot staff.

---

## Tier 2: Adjacent associations with public data

Same shape, different vertical. You do not need trucking proof for these, only
the live site as a demonstration that the machine works.

Categories to work, each with a provincial or national body in Canada:

- Other provincial trucking bodies: Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Atlantic,
  Quebec, Newfoundland. Same pitch, same industry, easiest second wave.
- Agriculture: grain growers, cattlemen, dairy, pork. All publish price and
  volume data members check constantly.
- Energy and mining: provincial bodies in AB, SK, BC.
- Construction and forestry: provincial associations, both track materials
  and input costs.
- Fisheries and ports: landing values, throughput, wait times.
- Aviation and rail: freight rates, on-time performance.

Find them the same way for each: search "[province] [industry] association",
then look for a page where they publish data. If they publish data in a PDF,
they are a candidate.

---

## Tier 3: B2B and trade publishers

They buy this because traffic and authority are their business.

| Organisation | Note |
|---|---|
| FleetOwner | Confirmed resource page, covers Canadian freight |
| Bulk Transporter | Covers bulk freight, fuel-sensitive audience |
| Annex Business Media | Canadian B2B publisher, many trade titles |
| Glacier FarmMedia | Canadian agriculture publisher |
| Newcom Media | Canadian trucking and supply chain titles |

Publishers are the best Tier 3 fit: they already have the audience, they lack
the data property, and they have a budget line for content.

---

## Tier 4: Companies that want category authority

The longest sales cycle and the biggest cheques. Look for companies whose
marketing already claims authority they cannot evidence.

Signals that they are a fit:
- They publish an "industry report" that is really a survey of their own users
- They publish a chart with no source on it
- They sponsor an award
- Their competitors have a data page and they do not
